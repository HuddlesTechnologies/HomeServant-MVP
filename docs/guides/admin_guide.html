1. What the admin console is

The admin console is the staff side of HomeServant. From it, admins look after the people and content on the platform: tenants, landlords and vendors, property listings, marketplace products and orders, reports raised by users, and "Contact Support" conversations.

It's part of the same web app as everything else, but it has its own sign-in page and only accounts with the Admin role can get in. Admin accounts are never created through the normal Sign Up screens: a super admin invites them from inside the console (see section 7).

Everything is checked on the server. Hiding a button in the console is only a convenience. Every action is checked again by the server against the admin's level, so an admin can't do more than their level allows even with a modified app.

2. Signing in and staying secure

Where to sign in

Open the website and go to the admin sign-in page: your site's address followed by /#/admin-login, for example https://homeservant.online/#/admin-login. Sign in with your admin email and password.

Admin accounts can only sign in there. The tenant, landlord and vendor sign-in pages refuse them, and so does "Continue with Google" (it would skip your password and two-factor code). Tenant, landlord and vendor accounts can't sign in on the admin page.

Your first sign-in

  1. When a super admin invites you, you get one email containing a 4-digit confirmation code and a temporary password.
  2. Give the code to the super admin who invited you. They type it into the console to create your account. The code expires after 10 minutes.
  3. Sign in with the temporary password. The console will not let you do anything else until you set your own password.

Security features

  • Two-factor authentication (2FA): turn it on under Account Settings (the gear icon at the top of the console). Each sign-in then also needs a code emailed to you. All admins should keep it on.
  • Automatic sign-out: the console signs you out after 15 minutes without activity.
  • No "Forgot password" for admins: admin accounts can't reset their own password from the login page. A locked-out admin must ask a Moderator or Super Admin to reset it (see section 7). This stops someone who has access to an admin's email inbox from taking over the account on their own.
  • Instant loss of access: if an admin is demoted or removed, the change applies on their very next action. They don't keep access until their session runs out.

3. Admin levels and what each can do

There are three levels. Each one includes everything the level below it can do.

4. A tour of the console

The bottom bar has four main tabs plus More. Red number badges show items that need attention.

Dashboard

Headline numbers (total users, tenants, landlords, vendors, properties, bookings, marketplace orders, pending vendor reviews, deactivated accounts) and a live activity feed. Tapping a card or a feed item jumps to the relevant list.

Users

Everyone on the platform, filterable by All, Tenants, Landlords, Vendors, Deactivated. Tap a user to open their detail page:

  • Profile details, which every admin can edit.
  • Their listings, with photos, and their bookings.
  • Actions, depending on your level: message them, change email, send a password reset, set a password, disable 2FA, deactivate, delete permanently.

Deactivating or deleting needs a written reason of at least 10 characters. It's emailed to the user and recorded in the Activity Log.

Vendors

Marketplace shop applications and shops, filterable by All, Pending, Approved, Rejected. Moderators and above can approve, reject (with a reason), suspend and unsuspend.

Reports

Problems users have reported about a property or a marketplace item, filterable by Open, In Progress, Resolved. Open a report to read it, move it through those statuses, or transfer it to another admin.

More

  • Properties: every listing. Moderators and above can remove one (with a reason) or re-list it.
  • Marketplace: products and orders. Moderators and above can remove a product (with a reason). Everyone can open an order's details.
  • Messages: your Inbox (conversations you're in) and the shared Support Queue (support chats nobody has taken yet). See section 5.
  • Admins (Moderator and above): the admin list. Super admins also invite, promote or demote, remove, and toggle 2FA here. See section 7.
  • Activity Log (Moderator and above): a record of sensitive actions, such as admin sign-ins, account deactivations and deletions, password and email changes, chat claims and transfers. Only a super admin can clear it.
  • Chat Log (Super Admin): every support conversation from the last 30 days, with who handled it and every transfer. Filter by Unattended, Opened, Resolved.

Top bar

The bell opens your notifications, the gear opens Account Settings, and the door icon signs you out.

5. Support chats

When a user taps Contact Support → Live Chat, they first pick what it's about (Payments, Booking, Account, Listing, Marketplace or Other), and a support conversation starts. Here's how it moves through the team:

  1. Auto-assigned: when the customer sends a message, the chat goes straight to an admin who is On duty and online, whoever has the fewest open chats (ties rotate). You're notified like any new message. If nobody on duty is online, it waits in the queue instead. A super admin can switch auto-assignment off (server setting SUPPORT_AUTO_ASSIGN=false).
  2. Unclaimed: it appears in Messages → Support Queue for every admin, and every admin gets one notification.
  3. Claimed: the first admin to open it from the queue, or to reply, takes it on. It leaves everyone else's queue and their notifications clear. From then on only that admin is notified of new messages.
  4. Resolved: the handling admin taps Mark as Resolved. The conversation disappears from the user's inbox; if they need help again, a fresh conversation starts.
  5. Ended by the customer: the customer can also tap End chat. It ends the same way as a resolve: a notice appears in the chat, you're notified if you were handling it, and nobody can send more messages there. If they need help again, a fresh conversation starts. It counts as resolved on Support Insights, which also shows how many were ended by customers. Customers are asked to rate a conversation only if an admin replied.

Tools inside a support chat

The admin handling a chat, and any super admin, get these at the top of the chat (on a phone they're under the headset icon):

  • Customer details: who they are, their recent bookings (or bookings on their listings), recent payments, reports, and their other support chats.
  • Internal notes: notes for other admins, never shown to the customer. Useful before a transfer.
  • Topic & priority: set what it's about and how urgent it is (Low, Normal, High, Urgent). The queue shows the most urgent first, and the inbox and queue show both as badges.
  • Handling history: who took it up and every hand-off.
  • Saved replies (the lightning icon by the message box): shared ready-made answers. Tap one to drop it into your reply and adjust it before sending. Anyone can add one; the author, moderators and super admins can edit or delete it.

Your sent messages show Sending… until they arrive, and Not sent · Tap to retry if they fail.

If nobody is available

  • The customer is told once, in the chat, that nobody is available right now, that they're in the queue, and that they'll get a notification (and an email if they're away) when someone replies.
  • Every minute, waiting chats are handed to anyone who has come online and on duty, most urgent and oldest first. Turning On duty on does it straight away. The admin gets "A waiting conversation was assigned to you".
  • After 5 minutes unclaimed, every admin gets a reminder. After 15 minutes, every super admin gets an alert and an email. Both happen once per chat. (A claimed chat whose customer has waited 10 minutes for a reply also alerts super admins, as before.)

Support Insights (Super Admin)

More → Support Insights shows how support is doing over the last 7, 30 or 90 days:

  • Right now: chats waiting for an admin, the longest wait, admins on duty and online, and open chats being handled.
  • Totals: conversations, % resolved, time to first reply (median, and the time 9 in 10 are answered within), time to resolve, customer rating, transfers, and chats never answered.
  • Charts: conversations per day and busiest hours (Lagos time). Hover or long-press a bar for its numbers.
  • By topic and by admin (open now, first replies, resolved, reply time, rating), plus the latest customer ratings and comments.

Customers are asked to rate a chat (1–5 stars, optional comment) when it's resolved. These figures are kept even after chat messages are deleted at 30 days. Use the per-admin numbers to plan cover and support people, not to rank them.

ID verification (Moderator and above)

Every tenant and landlord gives a means of ID and its number at signup; landlords also upload a certificate of ownership and a supporting document. These are kept private. More → ID Verifications lists submissions (badge = waiting for review); the list only shows the last 4 digits of each ID number. Tap one to open the user's details, where the Identity Verification card shows the full number and the documents.

  • Images preview in place; tap to open full size. PDFs open with Open PDF. Links are private and expire after 10 minutes (they refresh when you open them).
  • Verify when the name, number and documents match the person. Reject needs a note saying what to fix; the user is notified and emailed with it.
  • If the user changes their details later, the submission goes back to Awaiting review.
  • Support-level admins see that the card exists but not its contents. Accounts created before this was required have no documents.

Verified badges

Once you verify someone, they get a blue Verified check: on every listing from a verified landlord (card and property page), next to verified tenants in a landlord's My Tenants, in the console's user list and user details, and as a status card on the person's own profile (verified, in review, or what to fix).

Platform Controls (Super Admin)

More → Platform Controls holds platform-wide switches. Only super admins can open it.

  • Only verified landlords: when on, tenants only see, search and book listings from landlords whose identity is verified. The screen shows how many listings would be hidden before you switch it on, and who last changed it.
  • Landlords always see their own listings; an unverified landlord sees a notice on their dashboard and profile that their listings are hidden until they're verified, with a Get verified button (accounts from before ID documents were required can submit them there, and rejected ones can resubmit).
  • Switching it on notifies and emails every unverified landlord who has listings, telling them what to do next. Tenants are not notified (to avoid alarm); they only see the note on the listing.
  • Pay unverified landlords (a separate switch, ON by default): ON — landlords are paid as normal on move-in, shortlet payment and renewal, verified or not. OFF — landlords must be verified before they are paid: payouts to unverified landlords are held by HomeServant and each landlord is told to get verified. The tenant's side (moving in, the stay, the renewal) is never held up. Held money is sent automatically when the landlord is verified, or to everyone when you switch it back ON. The card shows how much is held right now. Verified landlords are always paid.
  • Existing tenancies, bookings and payments are not affected. Tenants who already booked or saved a hidden listing still see it, with a note: "This listing has been hidden because the landlord is unverified" (on the History card, the property page and saved listings), and that their booking carries on.

Eviction requests (Super Admin)

A landlord can ask to end a tenancy early, with a written reason. Every super admin is notified, and the case waits in More → Eviction Requests (the badge shows how many are waiting). Each case shows the property, both parties' contact details, the lease dates, the landlord's reason and the tenant's response, if they have given one.

  • Approve eviction ends the lease today and puts the property back on the market. An optional note goes to both parties. This can't be undone.
  • Reject needs a reason (both parties see it); the tenancy continues unchanged.
  • Give the tenant a fair chance to respond before deciding, and contact either side through Messages if you need more information. Only one super admin can decide a case; if someone else already did, you're told.
  • The case stays on record with who decided, when and why (filter by Approved, Rejected or Withdrawn).

Transferring and reassigning

The transfer list shows every admin's level (Support, Moderator, Super Admin), whether they're on duty and online, and how many open chats they have. Filter by level or to on-duty admins only, so the chat goes to someone who can actually help. The most available people are listed first. The customer sees a notice in the chat: "This conversation has been transferred to Ada from HomeServant Support" (first name only).

  • Transfer (the ⇄ icon in the chat): the handling admin hands the chat to another admin. The new admin is notified in the app and by email, and the old admin loses access.
  • Reassign (super admins only): for a chat stuck with an admin who's away, or one nobody has picked up. A super admin opens it from the Chat Log or from a notification and taps Reassign. Both the new and previous admin are notified.
  • If the chat is taken away from you while you have it open, the reply box is replaced by a note saying who has it now.
  • Handling history: once a chat has changed hands, the admin now handling it (and any super admin) sees a banner at the top, such as "Transferred to you by Ada", with who first took it up. The clock icon, or History, opens the full timeline: who took it up, every transfer and reassignment with who made it and when, and who has it now. The customer doesn't see this.

Opening chats from notifications

Tapping a message notification shows where that conversation stands now: "Waiting for an admin", "You are handling this", "Transferred to Bola", or "Resolved 2h ago". It then offers Reply if you still can, or View conversation (read-only) if you're allowed to look but not reply.

Exporting a transcript

Inside a chat, the export action saves the conversation as a PDF.

6. Alerts, sounds and browser pop-ups

How alerts work

  • A new support conversation gives each admin one alert. Further messages before anyone takes it update that same alert quietly ("Ada (3 messages): …").
  • As soon as someone takes the chat, the alert clears for everyone else.
  • Reminder after 5 minutes: if nobody has picked up a new conversation, all admins get one "Support conversation still waiting" alert.
  • Escalation after 10 minutes: if the customer's latest message in a claimed chat has gone unanswered for 10 minutes, super admins get one "Customer waiting for a reply" alert and can reassign it.
  • Both times can be changed without touching code. See the SUPPORT_UNCLAIMED_REMINDER_MINUTES and SUPPORT_REPLY_ESCALATION_MINUTES settings in the Backend Developer Guide.

Your settings (gear icon → Account Settings)

SettingWhat it does
On dutyOn: you hear a sound for new support conversations. Off ("Away"): they still arrive, silently. Chats you're handling always alert you. This setting follows you to any device.
Alert sounds on this deviceTurns sounds on or off for this computer or phone only. A device never plays more than one alert sound in 10 seconds.
Browser pop-upsShows alerts as a pop-up from your browser when the console tab isn't in view (another tab in front, or the window minimised). Your browser asks for permission the first time you turn it on. Clicking a pop-up brings the console back and opens that notification.
Sounds and pop-ups need the console to be open in a browser tab, even a background one. If the tab or browser is closed, alerts wait in your notification list until you sign in.
The server runs on a plan that can go to sleep when nobody is using the app. Reminders can't be sent while it's asleep; overdue ones go out as soon as it wakes.

7. Managing other admins

All of this is in More → Admins.

Moderators and super admins can open the Admins page. Search by name or email, and filter by level (Super Admins, Moderators, Support). It shows a headcount at the top (all admins, then super admins, moderators and support), and each admin's account creation date in the list. Tap any admin to see which super admin invited them. Only super admins can invite, remove or change an admin's level. Admins created before this was recorded, the very first admin, and admins promoted directly in the database show "Not recorded".

Inviting a new admin (Super Admin)

  1. Tap New Admin and enter their full name, email and level, then Send Code.
  2. They receive one email with a 4-digit confirmation code and a temporary password.
  3. Ask them for the code and enter it in the console within 10 minutes. Their account is created.
  4. They sign in with the temporary password and must set their own straight away.

If you close the code screen by accident, tapping New Admin again within 10 minutes offers to continue that invite. After 10 minutes the code has expired, so start over (the form is pre-filled).

Changing an admin's level (Super Admin)

Pick the new level next to the admin's name. It applies immediately. You can't demote the last super admin: promote someone else first.

Resetting an admin's password (Moderator and above)

  1. Choose Reset password on the locked-out admin.
  2. A confirmation code is sent to your own email, which proves it's really you at the keyboard. Enter it.
  3. The admin gets a new temporary password by email and is signed out everywhere.

A Moderator can't reset a Super Admin's password.

Removing an admin (Super Admin)

This deletes their account permanently. You can't remove yourself or the last super admin.

Admin two-factor authentication (Super Admin)

A super admin can turn 2FA on or off for any admin. Keep it on for everyone.

8. Day-to-day moderation

A user asks for help with their account

Find them under Users. Support can correct their name, phone or address. For email or password problems, a Moderator can change the email, send a reset email, or (as a last resort) set a password directly.

A vendor applies to open a shop

Look at the application under Vendors → Pending, then approve it or reject it with a clear reason. Suspending a vendor hides their products until they're unsuspended.

A listing or product breaks the rules

Remove it with a reason (the owner is told why). A removed property can be re-listed later if it's fixed.

A user reports a property or product

Open it under Reports and set it to In Progress while you look into it. Transfer it if someone else should handle it, then set it to Resolved. While a report about a marketplace item is open, that sale's payment is held and not automatically released to the vendor.

Someone needs to be removed

Deactivate hides the account and signs it out; the user can come back by signing in again within 30 days. Delete permanently cannot be undone. Both need a reason, which is emailed to the user.

9. Emergency: the console has been taken over

Use this if an admin account has been stolen, an unknown admin appears, or you're locked out of every super admin account. You'll need access to the two services the platform runs on: Render (where the server runs) and Supabase (where the database is). Those two logins are the real master keys to HomeServant. Keep them with the owners only, protected by 2FA.

Step 1: Sign everyone out, everywhere

Changing the secret keys that sign login sessions makes every existing session invalid instantly, including the attacker's.

  1. In Render, open the homeservant-api service → Environment.
  2. Replace the values of JWT_ACCESS_SECRET and JWT_REFRESH_SECRET with two new long random strings. On a Mac or Linux terminal, openssl rand -hex 32 generates one; run it twice.
  3. Save. Render redeploys the server, and every user and admin has to sign in again.
  4. For good measure, in Supabase → SQL Editor, run:
UPDATE "RefreshToken" SET "revokedAt" = now() WHERE "revokedAt" IS NULL;

Step 2: Create a new super admin you control

Option A (normal case): promote a fresh account through the database.

  1. On the normal website, sign up with an email address only you control. Enter the verification code; you don't need to finish the profile steps. This creates an ordinary account.
  2. In Supabase → SQL Editor, run the following, putting your email in:
UPDATE "User"
SET "role" = 'ADMIN',
    "adminLevel" = 'SUPER_ADMIN',
    "mustChangePassword" = false,
    "deactivatedAt" = NULL
WHERE "email" = 'you@yourdomain.com';
  1. Sign in at /#/admin-login with that email and the password you chose at sign-up. You're now a super admin.
  2. Turn on 2FA straight away under Account Settings.

Option B (only if no admin accounts exist at all): the server has a one-time "bootstrap" route that creates the first super admin. It refuses to work while any admin exists, so it's only useful if every admin account has been deleted. It needs the ADMIN_BOOTSTRAP_SECRET value from Render:

curl -X POST https://homeservant-api-c8w7.onrender.com/api/admin/bootstrap \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -H "x-admin-bootstrap-secret: <ADMIN_BOOTSTRAP_SECRET from Render>" \
  -d '{"email":"you@yourdomain.com","password":"a-long-new-password","fullName":"Your Name"}'

Step 3: Remove the intruder

Signed in as your new super admin, open More → Admins:

  • Remove any admin you don't recognise.
  • Reset the password of every admin you do keep, and check their 2FA is on.

If you can't use the console, you can demote an account directly in the database. It loses admin access on its very next request:

UPDATE "User" SET "role" = 'TENANT', "adminLevel" = NULL
WHERE "email" = 'suspicious@example.com';

Step 4: Check what was done

  • Activity Log: look for account deletions, email or password changes, and 2FA being turned off during the incident. Contact affected users.
  • Payouts: check that landlords' and vendors' bank details weren't changed.
  • Chat Log: look for messages sent to users in the platform's name.

Step 5: Close the door

  • In Render, also replace ADMIN_BOOTSTRAP_SECRET with a new random value.
  • If you think the Render or Supabase accounts themselves were reached:
    • change those passwords and turn on 2FA there;
    • rotate the Supabase service role key and database password (update SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY, DATABASE_URL and DIRECT_URL in Render);
    • rotate the Paystack secret key and the Resend email API key.
  • Write down what happened and when, for your records.
